# Day 37 / 60 — LinkedIn Post Day 37 of 60 🧭 Nine real decisions. Four health systems. One CEO simulation. I rebuilt today's challenge into a walkthrough of Task Compass: Leadership Crossroads, a decision simulation set inside a critical access hospital in the Midwest, one in Oman, and large academic medical centers in the US and Saudi Arabia. Same three stages every time: who owns it, how does it route through the leadership team, who has to be in the room when it's bigger than one person. Some of what's actually inside it: who owns a Medicare Cost Report deadline before the hospital loses its reimbursement status. Who owns the only dialysis specialist walking out the door. Who owns an EHR migration that's gone sideways at a teaching hospital. Who's in the room when physician burnout hits three departments at once, on two different continents, in two different languages. Three things this build kept teaching me: 01 | Ownership follows proximity to the problem, not seniority. The closest operational leader usually owns it, whether the title is CNO or Director of Nursing, CFO or Director of Finance and MOH Liaison. 02 | The bigger the crisis, the more it needs a room, not a hero. Every "collaboration challenge" scenario in this build needed three or four leaders at the table, never one. 03 | Bilingual only counts if it's structural. Full right-to-left mirroring for the Oman and Saudi Arabia scenarios, not translated labels sitting inside an English layout.
